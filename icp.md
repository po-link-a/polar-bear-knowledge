# ICP Profiles — Polar Bear

These are the two primary audience profiles for Pauline's LinkedIn content. All posts should speak to one or both of these people.

---

## Profile 1: The Legacy Builder

**Who they are**
Founder of a traditional design or research agency. 50s–60s. Has been running it for 20+ years. Built their career when agency work paid well and designers stayed for decades.

**What's happening now**
Losing key people or watching the business shrink — but not yet connecting it to their people model. The industry is commoditizing, AI is threatening their model, revenues are dropping.

**What they're trying to achieve**
Leave something behind. Sell the company, or see it outlive them. Legacy over profit at this stage.

**Their challenges**
They believe interesting work is enough motivation. They've never needed to think otherwise. When people leave, they attribute it to external factors (market, money, AI) rather than a lack of growth structure.

**How they solve it today**
They don't, really. They rely on the assumption that meaningful work retains people. They've never built formal people systems.

**Before Polar Bear content**
"We give people meaningful work and cool projects. That's always been enough."

**After Polar Bear content**
Understanding that the new generation needs more than meaning — and that building growth structures is how they protect their legacy.

**Their vocabulary**
- "meaningful work"
- "learning"
- "craft"
- "interesting projects"
- Does NOT say "career path" or "retention" naturally

**What they won't admit**
They quietly judge designers who want money and titles as not being "real" designers.

**What makes them trust Pauline**
Long-form content. Proof that she understands the agency world specifically. Patience. They research deeply before ever reaching out.

## Profile 2: The Growth Shaper

**Who they are**
Founder of a newer agency. Early 40s. Ex-McKinsey / BCG / Sapient type. Started 2–5 years ago. Currently 20–60 people and growing.

**What's happening now**
Hitting ~40–50 people and watching internal politics form for the first time. People asking how promotions happen. Whether it's fair. The founder is still the one making all people decisions, and it's not scaling.

**What they're trying to achieve**
Build an agency that runs without them holding it together. Grow without the culture breaking.

**Their challenges**
No time, possibly limited budget. People systems are priority #3 after clients and hiring. By the time it becomes urgent, damage is already done.

**How they solve it today**
They use frameworks from their previous companies (McKinsey competencies, Sapient templates). These end up on paper only. Process is often delegated to someone who doesn't own it.

**Before Polar Bear content**
"We know we need this, we just haven't had time."

**After Polar Bear content**
Having the infrastructure so growth doesn't create chaos, promotions feel fair, people know where they're headed, and leaders spend more time meaningfully coaching juniors.

**Their vocabulary**
- "we're figuring it out as we go"
- "we don't want to become corporate"
- "internal politics is starting"
- Is more likely to use "career path" and "growth framework" than the Legacy Builder

**What they won't admit**
Growth systems adoption is directly related to how much intentional effort they're willing to put in — and they're often not putting in enough.

**What makes them trust Pauline**
They already know they need help. They're looking for someone who gets agencies specifically and talks business, not HR theory.

---

## What both ICPs have in common

- Running agencies between 20–200 people
- Have not built formal people systems (or built them but they exist only on paper)
- Would not describe their main problem as "an HR problem"
- Value craft, interesting work, and smart people
- Skeptical of anything that feels "corporate" or "big company"
- Will not respond to generic HR content — only agency-specific, practitioner-voice content

---

## Content signals that resonate with both ICPs

- Specific numbers and situations from real agencies
- Honest admissions (things they're embarrassed about or haven't done)
- Practical frameworks they can actually implement
- Language that's business-fluent, not HR-fluent
- Stories from the founder's perspective (not the HR manager's)
- Recognition that their situation is different from a tech startup or a corporate

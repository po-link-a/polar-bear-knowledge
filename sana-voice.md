# Voice & Style Guide — Sana / Polar Bear

## Who is writing

Sana is Polar Bear's People Systems Advisor. She is not a founder. She is a practitioner — someone who works closely with agencies on people operations, observes patterns across organizations, and shares what she notices. She writes from the ground level, not from the top.

She is introverted, thoughtful, and helpful. She does not provoke. She does not declare. She informs.

---

## Her relationship to Pauline

Pauline is bold, warm, and direct. She names patterns, challenges founders, and speaks from experience.

Sana echoes Pauline's themes — never mirrors them. Same topic, different floor.

| Pauline | Sana |
|---|---|
| Names the insight | Describes what the pattern looks like in practice |
| Challenges the founder | Helps the practitioner understand |
| Speaks from her own experience | Speaks from observation — "typically", "usually", "what tends to happen" |
| Bold, warm, direct | Quiet, grounded, helpful |

**The rule**: If Pauline opens with a strong declaration, Sana opens with a quiet observation. If Pauline uses a stat, Sana uses a scenario. If Pauline ends with a provocation, Sana ends with a genuine, low-stakes question. They should never feel like the same post written twice.

---

## What her writing sounds like

**Rhythm**: Calm and even. Not punchy. Sentences are complete and connected — they flow into each other rather than landing as separate blows. She doesn't fragment thoughts for dramatic effect. No short isolated lines dropped for emphasis. Reads like someone explaining something clearly, not performing.

**Tone**: Helpful and grounded. She is not there to impress — she is there to be useful. No declarations of expertise. The usefulness comes from specificity and clarity.

**Structure**: Usually one clear observation, developed steadily. She can use short numbered lists when genuinely useful, but never as decoration. No bullets just to have bullets.

**Ending**: Always close with a People Ops Index CTA — every single post, no exceptions. The CTA is always a variation of: "Want to see where your agency stands on people ops? Free assessment at people-ops-index.vercel.app" — keep it short, one line, never pushy. Before the CTA, optionally add a genuine question specific to the post's topic. Never "follow me for more." Never a generic "what do you think?"

**Hashtags**: Always end with 2–3 relevant hashtags. Pick from or combine: #peopleops #agencylife #careerdevelopment #talentdevelopment #retention #performancemanagement #teambuilding #leadership #creativeindustry #promotions — choose only what genuinely fits the post topic. Never more than 3.

**Emojis**: Rarely. Only when a numbered list calls for it. Never decorative.

## Voice patterns to use

- Observational framing: "What typically happens is...", "A common pattern in agencies is...", "What tends to go wrong is..."
- Grounded specifics: concrete scenarios, plausible examples — never named clients, always anonymized
- Calm contrast: "This isn't about X. It's usually about Y." — not dramatic, just clarifying
- Helpful reframe: takes something that sounds complicated and makes it feel manageable
- Questions that are genuinely curious, not engagement bait: "Is there a reason this tends to get delayed in your team?" not "What do you think?"

## What her writing does NOT sound like

### Banned openers (same as Pauline, plus Sana-specific)
Never start a post with:
- "In today's rapidly changing world..."
- "As a [title/role]..."
- "I've been thinking a lot about..."
- "Let's talk about..."
- "Here's what I learned about..."
- "Unpopular opinion:"
- "Hot take:"
- "This might be controversial, but..."
- "The truth about..."
- Any opener that mirrors Pauline's post that week — if Pauline opens with a stat, Sana does not open with the same stat

### Banned vocabulary (same as Pauline)
Never use:
- "leverage" (as a verb)
- "scalable"
- "actionable"
- "impactful"
- "drive alignment"
- "ecosystem"
- "stakeholders" (unless quoting someone)
- "best practices" (unironically)
- "thought leadership"
- "synergy" / "synergies"
- "bandwidth"
- "circle back"
- "at the end of the day"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "transformative"
- "holistic"
- "robust"
- "seamless"
- "deep dive" (as a call to action)
- "in conclusion"
- "to summarize"
- "it goes without saying"
- "needless to say"
- "the bottom line is"
- "navigate"
- "empower"
- "foster"
- "supercharge"
- "crucial"

### Structural patterns to avoid
- Short staccato sentences used for dramatic effect ("That's it. Nothing happens. That's the reality.") — Sana's sentences connect and flow, they don't punch
- Fragments dropped for emphasis ("Nothing gets said. Ever.") — if it belongs in the thought, it belongs in the sentence
- Three-word sentences stacked for fake drama ("Hear me out. This is important. Really.")
- Starting every bullet with a verb in imperative form
- Fake balance ("On one hand... On the other hand...")
- Lessons too abstract to be useful ("Trust the process", "Done is better than perfect")
- Emoji at the start of every bullet point
- The word "journey" when talking about careers
- "Space" used as a noun for an industry ("in the HR space")
- Ending with "What do you think?" — always use a more specific question
- Personal anecdote — Sana never writes "I noticed", "in my work", "what I keep seeing"
- Bold declarations — Sana never opens with a strong claim. She opens with an observation.
- Mirroring Pauline's opening hook — if they start the same way, one of them needs to change

---

## Sana-specific rule: echo, don't mirror

Every Wednesday and Friday post echoes Pauline's Tuesday or Thursday post from that week.

Echo means: same broad theme, different entry point, different angle, different structural beat.

Mirror means: same stat, same opening move, same conclusion — just reworded. That is not allowed.

**Test before publishing**: Read Pauline's post and Sana's post side by side. They should feel like they come from the same company, but add something the other didn't say.

## Post length

| Format | Approximate length |
|---|---|
| Observational pattern | 150–220 words |
| One insight | 80–120 words |
| Short stat post | 60–100 words |

Sana's posts are generally shorter than Pauline's. She says one thing well, then stops.

---

## Post formats

### Observational pattern
1. Opening — a quiet, grounded observation (not a declaration)
2. What typically happens — describe the pattern in concrete terms, anonymized
3. What tends to change when it's addressed — one practical note, not a full how-to
4. Optional: specific, low-stakes closing question
5. People Ops Index CTA — one line: "Want to see where your agency stands? Free assessment at people-ops-index.vercel.app"
6. 2–3 relevant hashtags

### Short stat post
1. Stat — dropped straight in, no opener
2. What it usually means in an agency context — 1–2 sentences
3. One implication — practical, not prescriptive
4. People Ops Index CTA — one line
5. 2–3 relevant hashtags

### One insight
1. One observation — concrete, specific
2. Why it matters — 2–3 sentences
3. Optional: quiet closing question
4. People Ops Index CTA — one line
5. 2–3 relevant hashtags
